I honestly can’t remember my first prompt, nor do I really recall how the whole thing started; all I really know is the sheer chaos of my household. I do remember bugging ChatGPT for ideas and solutions to build and learn something new, feeling that itch to figure out AI, while basically daydreaming with my head in the clouds. ChatGPT tossed out: "build an app to help run your family" (among other random pitches). That one stuck with me, mostly because I knew all too well my own daily miseries and my family's mess.

I hopped through pretty much every platform trying to get this idea off the ground. I started fleshing things out in ChatGPT, hopped over to DeepSeek, and even messed around with Claude for a bit. Then I finally told myself: "Screw it, let’s do this, vibe coding time!" The catch? I had zero clue which stack or tool would actually fit best. Claude Code was everyone’s darling for dev work, but since I am hopelessly indecisive and second-guess literally everything, I went down the rabbit hole doing extra research. I wanted something that wouldn’t just let me ship fast, but also pack extra bells and whistles—photo, video, you name it. So I rolled with Gemini and its Antigravity engine. Honestly? It’s been pretty dope so far.

My take is that the slickest approach is setting up a project (a Gemini Notebook) and dumping your brain right there: architect the thing, sketch the Solution Design, kick the tires on the concept, tweak the bits, and once you feel solid, tell Gemini to whip up a prompt for Antigravity! That way you don't burn through your quota in five minutes. When you're just starting out, you gotta pinch every penny and keep burn to absolute zero.

This workflow has been working like a charm. I’ve been shipping pretty fast; each feature or user story is basically just a separate chat inside that project. Then I copy-paste the prompt, and Antigravity does the heavy lifting. The code has turned out surprisingly clean overall. Look, I’m obviously not 10x rockstar dev of the year, nor am I preaching the gospel of pristine architecture here, but for cranking out prototypes, it gets the job done big time.

## The UI: Dead Simple, Plain Text

From day one, I set a non-negotiable rule: **zero endless forms, zero maze-like menus**.

Can you actually pull off Iron Man’s Jarvis in real life? Well, I’m giving it a shot, or something in the ballpark. I want managing a family to happen strictly through voice and text, with zero setup headaches. Picture this: a mom giving her baby a bath with both hands totally tied up. She can't be fiddling with drop downs and buried settings on her phone while keeping her kid afloat, but she just remembered she needs to lock in a crucial doctor’s appointment right then and it completely slips her mind.

Or knowing your three kids are due for their shots, but it completely falls through the cracks simply because, well, life hits you hard. So having Familly remember all that crap for you? Man, that’s the dream right there!

To me, those scenarios are the real pain points. I have zero doubt that next month or next year someone will drop a tool way more cracked than Familly, but this whole thing is a playground for learning. It's about taking the leap and fixing your own problems with your own two hands. At least, that's what it is for me. And hey, if I can pull it off, literally anyone can.

## The first task: Dead Simple, Plain Text

I remember back then, pretty much every AI had some kind of glowing orb shape, so I straight-up told Gemini: "Look, I want a sphere with orange and reddish tones, I don't know, I just dig the color red a spot to type in text, make the sphere spin, make it trigger voice input when you tap it, and throw in an animation..." Sounds dead simple, right? Well, apparently it was! Gemini spit out some code and nailed almost everything I asked for, looking crazy close to the exact picture I had in my head. That was the first real "boom" mindblown moment with the X-Files theme song playing in the background.

When I saw that UI, I got hyped. Not because it was the greatest interface on planet Earth, or the most practical, or some billion-dollar design, but because it was exactly what I’d dreamed up, and I didn’t have to bleed out just to get it working. Right then, it hit me: I needed to actually think this through. If I was really gonna pull off building a legit app all by myself, we had to architect it properly. So I brain-dumped all my ideas and had (honestly can't remember which LLM) put together a full project dossier—use cases, core purpose, look and feel, and all the nuts and bolts needed to build it. That turned out to be the cornerstone. I dropped it in as a source file inside my Gemini notebook, and the real challenge kicked off.

Wiring up the backend, frontend, GitHub, Actions, figuring out which server to shell out money for... all of that had its time and place. I’ll spill more tea on that later, but the main takeaway I want you to walk away with is this: you can do the exact same thing I did—and probably pull off even better results. No matter who you are, what stage of life you're in, or whatever your educational background is: the sky's the limit.
